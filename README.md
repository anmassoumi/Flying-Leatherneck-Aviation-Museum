# Flying-Leatherneck-Aviation-Museum

I will be collaborating with the Flying Leatherneck Aviation Museum to design and construct a full-scale, from-scratch replica of the Curtiss JN-4 “Jenny” biplane, an iconic World War I-era trainer used extensively by the U.S. Marine Corps. Unlike restoration projects, this effort involves recreating the entire airframe from the ground up using a combination of historical research, modern engineering tools, and traditional fabrication techniques. The goal is to produce a historically accurate yet structurally sound aircraft that can serve as a centerpiece for educational exhibits and public outreach.

My work is focused on the design, fabrication, and assembly of the fuselage. I interpret original engineering drawings and archival documentation to understand the structure and geometry of the “Jenny,” then translate that information into detailed CAD models using SolidWorks. These digital models guide our fabrication process and help identify structural challenges or areas requiring adaptation to meet current safety standards and display requirements. I assist in selecting appropriate materials, such as wood species that match the strength and appearance of original components, and in determining modern equivalents for outdated hardware and fittings.

In the shop, I will be cutting, shaping, and assembling the wooden fuselage framework using techniques that I have learned from other projects as well as learn new techniques if needed. This includes precise joinery, reinforcement of key load-bearing points, and alignment of the longerons, cross-members, and bulkheads to ensure the structural accuracy of the aircraft. I also collaborate with teammates working on the wings, tail, and support structures to ensure dimensional consistency and integration across subsystems.
